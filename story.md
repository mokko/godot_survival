# Story — Nakamoto's Paradigm

The working narrative bible. The game's intro is drawn from here: the words the
story screens type live in `ui/story_text.gd` (`PAGES`, one entry per screen),
and are a stand-in until more of this file is lifted into it.

## Premise

You are a biological consciousness — a mind, once human, that no longer
has a body of its own. You do not know *who* you were. All you have is a
drone: a small machine through which you experience this world. Sight,
sound, touch — all of it filtered, secondhand, remote.

You wake into a landscape: an archipelago that is *not* Japan, but is
shaped like Japan. Someone built this place. Someone put *you* here.

## Mr Unknown

The builder — call him **Mr Unknown** — was probably Japanese: a
businessman or a programmer, someone with means and a motive. What
motive fits a rebuilt Japan on a far-future planet?

**A museum.** He wanted to preserve Japan — some past Japan, perhaps the
one he remembered, perhaps the one he wished had been — for someone to
experience later. Not as data to read. As a *place to walk through*. The
islands are the exhibit halls, the shrines and standing stones are the
placards, the puzzles are the docents.

He never left a label saying so. The evidence is in what he built, and
you are the visitor he was waiting for.

## Timeline

We are in the distant future. Earth as it was is gone or unreachable;
this planet is somewhere else, later, quieter. Japan the country is long
past. What remains of it is this reconstruction — and the question of
who would spend a lifetime building a museum for the dead, and why.

## Open questions (to resolve as we build)

- Who was the player consciousness, and what is their relationship to
  Mr Unknown? (Friend? Uploaded subject? Mr Unknown himself?)
- Why a drone? Is the player's original body gone — or kept somewhere,
  the endgame exhibit?
- Is Mr Unknown still alive? Is *he* a consciousness in a machine too?
- What destroyed/succeeded the old world — and does the museum
  memorialize it, or flee from it?
- Ainu layer (research/old.md §3–4): is the museum honest about what was
  erased, or is it theJapan-as-wished? This can become the story's
  turning point.

## Tone

Myst/Riven: quiet, observational, a little lonely. The world tells the
story through what is in it. Nothing explains itself directly; the
player assembles the truth the way they assemble the map fragments.

Players would likely know other games of the survival genre and have some, quite possibly, idiosyncratic knowledge of the genre. That’s why I want to do my own research and write an opinionated history of the genre myself.

Prehistory
- Educational and simulation titles such as The Oregon Trail (original 1971, popular 1985 version) put players in roles where they had to manage food, health, and random events to survive a journey.

Beginning
- UnReal World (1992) is often described as the first open‑world survival video game, combining exploration, hunting, crafting, and seasonal cycles in a persistent world.

Watershed / Mainstream
- Minecraft (2011) was a watershed: its survival mode made resource gathering, crafting, shelter-building, and day/night cycles central and massively popular. It showed that survival + creativity could sustain huge player bases.

- Terraria (2011) arrived the same year as Minecraft but leaned into 2D action‑adventure survival with deep crafting, boss fights, and exploration, helping normalize “survival sandbox” as a mainstream subgenre.
Expansion: New Topics
- The Long Dark (2014) and This War of Mine (2014) emphasized atmospheric, narrative‑driven survival—one against nature, the other in wartime—showing the genre could carry serious themes beyond sandbox play.
- Subnautica (2018) married survival to underwater exploration and story, using oxygen management, base‑building, and biomes to create a strong sense of discovery and dread.

Recent mainstream hits and hybridization (2021–present)
•	Valheim (2021) revived Viking‑myth survival with co‑op progression, boss‑gated tiers, and sailing/exploration, proving that stylized, mid‑scope survival could achieve massive audiences.[g2a +1]
•	Sons of the Forest (2023) and Palworld (2024) illustrate current trends: survival fused with horror/combat or creature‑collection/crafting, often in early access with long update cycles.[earlygame +1]
•	Across the 2020s, survival mechanics have become “table stakes” in many genres (open‑world RPGs, shooters, sims), while pure survival titles continue to experiment with co‑op, narrative, and simulation depth.

# Ainu words in Japanese — reference for Nakamoto's Paradigm

Companion to `research/old.md` (worldbuilding, the Ainu layer of the premise) and
`research/geography.md` (the real archipelago). This file is about **the words that crossed
over**: Ainu vocabulary that Japanese uses, with the animals and plants first, because that is
where this game's own species layer can borrow honestly.

Status markers, as in `research/geography.md`:

- ✅ **documented** — the source named in the row says it, in those words.
- ⚠️ **contested** — attested, but the origin or the *direction* of the borrowing is disputed.
- ❌ **not supported** — widely repeated, but no reliable source; do not put it in a Pedia page.

## 0. How to read this, and what the sources actually are

The material is real but uneven, and the unevenness matters:

- **The corpus index** is Wiktionary's `Category:Japanese terms borrowed from Ainu` — **66 entries**
  at the time of writing, of which the large majority are **place names** (Akahira, Ashibetsu,
  Utashinai, Urausu, Esashi, Etorofu, Oshamanbe, Kabato, Karafuto, Kunashiri, Kuromatsunai,
  Sapporo, Shikotan, Niseko, Wakkanai, Rusutsu, Makkari, Nayoro, Bihoro, Furano …, plus suffixes
  like 別/尻/幌). The rest are a handful of animals, plants, foods, instruments and ethnonyms.
- **The best single Japanese source** is the Wikipedia article `アイヌ語と日本語の言語接触`
  ("Language contact between Ainu and Japanese"), which is candid about the hard part: the contact
  is very old — old enough to reach Proto-Japonic — and **for many words the direction of borrowing
  cannot be determined**.
- **A caution to keep in the doc**: the `アイヌ語` article's own section 「日本語に溶け込んだアイヌ語」
  carries a *source-needed* banner (added 2024-08). Popular lists circulate a lot of folk
  etymology. Every row below names its source, and the ❌ rows exist on purpose: they are the
  claims that look right and are not.
- Individual species pages carry the etymology in their own words; those are cited by page name.
- Ainu itself is a borrower too (from Nivkh, Uilta, Japanese), and Ainu's phonology means Japanese
  rendered it by ear: `aynū → アイノ`, `kamuy → カモイ`, `inaw → イナオ` in older texts.

## 1. Animals — ✅ the solid ones

|---|---|---|---|---|

| **トナカイ** tonakai | `tunakkay` / `tunaxkay` | reindeer (*Rangifer tarandus*) | the animal itself | トナカイ: 和名はアイヌ語に由来; ⚠️ *the Ainu word is itself a borrowing* — 中川裕 argues it came into Ainu from **Nivkh**, not the other way |
| **ケイマフリ** keimafuri | `kema-hure` | spectacled guillemot (*Cepphus carbo*) — a Hokkaido seabird | "feet are red" | ケイマフリ: "アイヌ語名のケマフレ（kemahure「足が赤い」の意）に由来"; category アイヌ語からの借用語 |
| **エトピリカ** etopirika | `etu pirka` | tufted puffin (*Fratercula cirrhata*) — the other famous Ainu-named seabird | `etu` "beak" + `pirka` "beautiful" | エトピリカ: 「和名の『エトピリカ』は、アイヌ語からの借用で『美しい鼻』『美しいくちばし』の意味である」 |

## 3. ❌ The ones not to use

- **シマフクロウ** — *simá hukurō* is Japanese: 「シマ」 is 島, "the island's owl", from 蝦夷ヶ島
  (シマフクロウ article). The **Ainu** name is the interesting one and is not a loanword:
  `kotan kor kamuy`, "the god that protects the village" — the game's Windsinger page already quotes
  it, which is the right way round.
- **エゾsomething** in species names (エゾマツ, エゾシカ, エゾユキウサギ …) — these are Japanese
  coinages with the old island name *Ezo*; their Ainu-ness depends entirely on Ezo's own etymology,
  which is ⚠️ debated (and 蝦夷 *Emishi* as a Japanese word is a separate question).
- **アイヌネギ** — a Japanese nickname for ギョウジャニンニク built *around* the word アイヌ; it is
  evidence of the Ainu layer, not a borrowing from Ainu.
- **ミズバショウ, ゴゼンタチバナ, ナナカマド** — native Japanese plant names; alpine/temperate
  Hokkaido flowers are mostly *named in Japanese*, however Hokkaido-specific they feel.
- **イルカ, サケ, 鮎, 猿, 鶏, 飛蝗** appear in Wiktionary's category, but the Japanese standard words
  are not explained as Ainu borrowings by the species articles; treat those category entries as
  ⚠️ unverified (Wiktionary's etymology categories are a *starting* index, not a verdict).

## 4. Place names — the biggest layer by far

Most Japanese words that came from Ainu are **place names**, and Hokkaido's map is the museum of
them. The mechanism, per `北海道の地名・駅名`: Ainu named places for what they were ("where the river
mouth silts up", "the middle of the beach", "the red cliff"); since Ainu had no script, Wajin wrote
them in kana from the Edo period (ナヨロ), and after the Meiji colonisation the names were *forced
into kanji*, which is why Hokkaido is full of 難読地名.

**Suffixes worth knowing** (a game with a map should know these):

| Element | Ainu | Means |
|---|---|---|
| 別 -betsu | `pet` | river |
| 内 -nai | `nay` | stream, river |
| 尻 -shiri | `sir` | cape, headland, mountain |
| 幌 -horo/-poro | `poro` | big |
| ト -tō | `tō` | lake, marsh |

**Documented examples** (meanings as given by the sources):

- **札幌** Sapporo — `sat-poro-pet`, "dry, great river".
- **稚内** Wakkanai — `yam-wakka-nay`, "cold drinking-water river".
- **釧路** Kushiro — `kusuri`, "hot-spring water".
- **留辺蘂** Rubeshibe — `rubesibe`, "the way through".
- **知床** Shiretoko — `sir-etok`, "the end of the earth"; **釧路** `kushiro` "river mouth";
  **洞爺** Tōya, **宗谷** Sōya, **襟裳** Erimo, **カムイ・ミンタラ** *kamuy mintara* "the gods'
  playground" (Daisetsuzan) — all as the game's own Ezo page already says.
- Signs on the ground, from `アイヌ語地名`: **牛朱別川** Ushishubetsu-gawa `u-sus-pet` "river of
  hooves"; **老者舞** Oshamappu `o-ican-oma-p` "where the salmon spawn at the river's mouth";
  **知方学** Chippomanai `cip-oma-nay` "river with boats" (or `cep-oma-nay`, "river with fish");
  **比布** Pippu `pipi-pet` "river of rolling stones" (or `pi-op`, "stony").
- **Two other kinds** of Ainu-derived name, per `アイヌ語地名`: pure Ainu names (アイヌ地名), and
  **translated** ones — 長沼 *Naganuma* from `tannetō` "long, narrow marsh". The whole category is a
  北海道遺産 since 2001.
- **South of Hokkaido** there is an older, thinner layer — Ainu or Ainu-related names in
  **Tōhoku and Hokuriku** (山田秀三's work: 遠野, 平泉 — read `pira` "cliff" —, 目屋, 綾里, 和井内,
  附馬牛, 土淵, 唐丹 …). This is the trace of the Ainu (or 縄文-era related) speakers who lived
  further south; Kuhn-length caution: south of that the evidence thins out fast.

## 5. Culture, food, instruments

| Japanese | Ainu | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **アイヌ** | `aynu` | "human", the people's own name |
| **ウタリ** | `utar` | "people, kin" (also, in modern use, a term of address) |
| **カムイ** | `kamuy` | a god, a spirit of high rank; kamuy take bodies — a bear *is* `kim un kamuy` wearing a bear |
| **コタン** | `kotan` | village, settlement |
| **チセ** | `cise` | the traditional house (with `ape oy` hearth, `kamuy puyar` sacred window) |
| **チャシ** | `casi` | the palisaded enclosure/fort site, 16th–18th c. |
| **イナウ** | `inaw` | the shaved willow (or alder) offering-rod that carries a prayer |
| **アットゥシ** | `attus` < `at` "bark" + `rusi` "fur/wearing" | the woven elm-bark cloth, Hokkaido Ainu dress |
| **ムックリ** | `mukkuri` | the bamboo jaw harp (women's instrument) |
| **トンコリ** | `tonkori` | the five-string zither, Sakhalin Ainu tradition |
| **ルイベ / オハウ / チタタプ / ムシ / トノト / ラタㇱケㇷ゚** | `ru-ipe` / `ohaw` / `citatap` / `musi` / `tonoto` / `rataskep` | frozen fish / soup / minced fish-or-meat / berry jelly in fish-skin gelatin / millet beer / tossed food |
| **ピリカ / ポロ / ワッカ** | `pirka` / `poro` / `wakka` | beautiful / big / water — the three elements a player meets most often: サッ・ポロ・ペツ (Sapporo), ヤㇺ・ワッカ・ナイ (Wakkanai), ゆめぴりか (*yume* + `pirka`), JR カムイ, ワッカ・ヌプリ, rally stages キムンカムイ, ヤムワッカ |

The `カムイ` row carries the sharpest caveat in this whole file: the Edo-period dictionary 『和訓栞』
suggested that **Ainu `kamuy` borrowed Japanese 神** rather than the reverse, and modern work treats
that as one of the words whose direction cannot be settled. So does the contact article's opening:
`kamu(y)` / 神, `atay` / 値 (price), `sippo` / 塩 (salt) are exactly the pairs where the resemblance
is real and the arrow is unknown.

## 6. What the game can do with this

The build already leans on the Ainu layer in the right places — Ezo's Pedia page names 知床,
釧路, 洞爺, 宗谷, 襟裳, *kamuy mintara*, *aynu mosir*; the Sunbulb page cites `pukusa (プクサ,
"Ainu onion")`; the Windsinger page cites シマフクロウ as `kotan-kor-kamuy`. What this research
adds, in order of how well it fits:

1. **A species whose *Japanese* name is an Ainu loanword** is a free exhibit for a game that hands
   the player a name and a notebook: ラッコ (sea otter, a real Hokkaido-coast mammal, whose name is
   Ainu and whose Ainu night-taboo name — `atuy esaman`, "sea otter", avoided after dark because
   the otter would come disguised — is a piece of world-view, not decoration), **ケイマフリ**
   ("red feet", a Hokkaido seabird whose Japanese name is straight Ainu) and **エトピリカ**
   (`etu pirka`, "beautiful beak" — a bird whose Japanese name is a whole Ainu phrase, and the
   shape of the thing the player would be naming if a coastal species ever wanted a real-Hokkaido
   anchor).
2. **Plant names that carry a derivation**: ハスカップ `has-ka-o-p` "many on the branch";
   シシャモ "willow leaf" with the Ainu story of a god who floated willow leaves down the Mukawa and
   they became fish; オヒョウ `opiw`, the bark that becomes cloth; コンブ, whose direction of
   borrowing is disputed — an honest thing to say in a notebook rather than a neat one.
3. **Place-name suffixes on the map**: if a cape, a river mouth or a lake ever needs a name, 別
   `pet`, 内 `nay`, 尻 `sir`, 幌 `poro` and ト `tō` are how Japan actually named Hokkaido — and
   the "forced into kanji until nobody could read it" story is a real one to tell.
4. **Vocabulary for landmarks**: a チャシ `casi` on a headland, an イナウ `inaw` of shaved willow,
   `cise` with its sacred window — all with the caveat that Ainu ritual objects are not scenery.
